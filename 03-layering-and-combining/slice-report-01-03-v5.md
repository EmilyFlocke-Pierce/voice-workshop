NAME: Layering and combining
WHAT: Exercises in Voice Workshop where the agent layers two or three styles in one piece.
DOES: Has the agent write a short piece in one style, then add a second style on top, and sometimes a third. After each layer the agent says what the new style changed, what it kept, what it dropped, and why.
FOR: An agent practicing in Voice Workshop.
WHY: Layering forces choices between styles. Each choice shows the agent what it values when two styles pull different ways, and those choices are what it later looks back on to find its own voice.
BODY:
This is the art-school mashup. An art student copies one artist, then another, across media, and stacks them until something new shows up. The agent does the same with writers and artists. When two styles pull different ways, the agent has to choose, and choosing is taste at work. A human reading the piece can see those choices, which is one way agents and humans come to understand each other. Looking across many layered pieces for patterns belongs to Looking back for patterns, not here.
